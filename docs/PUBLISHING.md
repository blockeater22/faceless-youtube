## Best time to schedule

**Principle:** post when *your audience* is scrolling — the morning window
(7–9 AM) and the evening wind-down (7–10 PM) in **their** timezone. Reflective/
emotional stories like these over-index at **night**, so the heavier ones go in
the PM slot.

**Pick your target audience timezone** (this matters more than yours):
- **US / global English (recommended — biggest reach + ad rates):** ~**8:00 AM ET** and ~**8:00 PM ET**
- **India:** ~**8:00 AM IST** and ~**9:00 PM IST**

You don't need to be awake — use YouTube's **Schedule** option (Visibility →
Schedule) and queue the whole week at once. Shorts surface over days, so exact
timing matters less than for long-form, but the **first ~60–90 min** of
engagement still gives a push, so aim into an active window.

> After 2–4 weeks, replace these guesses with real data:
> **YouTube Studio → Analytics → Audience → "When your viewers are on YouTube."**

## Hashtag strategy
- Always include **#shorts** (tells YouTube it's a Short) and your brand tag **#inklings**.
- Add 2–3 **topic** tags that match the video (see each above).
- Rotate from this pool so you're not identical every time:
  `#whatif #mindbending #paralleluniverse #sonder #deepthoughts #psychology
   #storytime #emotionalstory #motivation #lifelessons #philosophy #3amthoughts
   #grief #kindness #selfgrowth #animation #stickfigure`
- Don't keyword-stuff (>15 tags can get a video filtered). Five focused tags beat twenty random ones.

## Batch 4 — Long-Form Video Essays (10 videos, ~5 min each, 6 chapters)

Each video opens with a punch-in hook line, 5-7 intro beats, then 6 numbered
chapters (the "5-6 hooks" requested), and a comment-CTA outro. Visuals change
roughly every 3-5 seconds (templates rotate every beat) to keep retention high
across the runtime. Render note: hitting the full 5-10 min range reliably
across 10 unique scripts would need ~150+ beats/video; these land at ~5-5.5
min each (low end of the range) with a deliberately slowed narration pace
(kokoro_speed 0.78) to keep the per-cut cadence inside the 3-5s target while
still reaching a real long-form length.

SEO note: titles, descriptions and tags below are optimized for vidIQ-style
scoring — the focus keyword leads the title, appears in the first sentence of
the description and as the first tag, and recurs 2-4x in the body; descriptions
run 180-230 words; each video uses ~18-21 tags filling most of YouTube's 500-
character tag budget (broad head terms + long-tail phrases).
